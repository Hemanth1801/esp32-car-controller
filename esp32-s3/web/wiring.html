ESP32-S3 N16R8 + OV3660 + L298N

Two-board mode: connect the L298N only to the old ESP32. Use D25→IN1, D26→IN2, D27→IN3, D14→IN4, D32→ENA, D33→ENB and GND→GND. The S3 needs USB power and its camera ribbon, with motor GPIO wires disconnected. The photo diagram below applies to the alternative single-S3 motor configuration. The old ESP32 physical guide remains at localhost:8765/wiring.

Drive motor → OUT1/OUT2. Two-wire spring-return steering motor → OUT3/OUT4. Neither motor lead connects permanently to ground. With all power off, remove ENA/ENB jumpers before attaching GPIO41/42. Add 10 kΩ pull-downs from each of IN1–IN4, ENA and ENB to ground.

Power connections

  1. Power the ESP through one USB connector from regulated 5 V. Do not also feed its 5 V header. Identify USB-UART versus native USB using the board markings or device enumeration; the photograph alone does not establish the connector roles.
  2. Keep the separate L298N 5V-EN regulator jumper removed. Feed regulated 5 V to its 5V logic terminal. This jumper is different from ENA/ENB.
  3. Your existing 5 V motor supply goes to the driver terminal marked +12V / VS. The terminal label does not require using 12 V. Join ESP GND, L298N GND and supply negatives; route motor current directly through the power wiring.
  4. At the driver, fit 100 µF bulk plus 100 nF ceramic supply decoupling with suitable voltage ratings. Verify the module has its flyback diodes. Motor current and the L298N voltage drop determine performance at 5 V.

A 5 V, 2 A bank's ability to start both motors remains unverified. Software heartbeats cannot prevent a reset from a supply dip.

Camera ribbon and reserved pins

Connect OV3660 using the onboard ribbon socket with power off. Check the actual socket/contact orientation before inserting it; do not force the latch. No external camera GPIO wires are needed.

Expected S3-EYE profile: SDA 4, SCL 5, XCLK 15, VSYNC 6, HREF 7, PCLK 13, D0–D7 = 11/9/8/10/12/18/17/16. The photo establishes header positions but not ribbon routing. Verify sensor identity and a JPEG with motor power off. Keep USB19/20, UART TX/RX, PSRAM35–37, boot pins0/3/45/46 and RGB48 free.

Upload and open

  1. Keep motor power off. Build with powershell -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File .\build-firmware.ps1 from the S3 folder. Arduino IDE equivalent: ESP32S3 Dev Module, 16MB flash, QIO 80MHz, OPI PSRAM, 16M Flash (3MB APP/9.9MB FATFS), upload115200.
  2. List serial ports using Arduino CLI or Device Manager. Upload with .\build-firmware.ps1 -Port COMx using the actual S3 port. The script verifies ESP32-S3 before writing; it refuses a classic ESP32. Use -NativeUSB only when choosing the native USB connection. BOOT/RESET may be needed.
  3. Open serial115200. Verify PSRAM, camera PID0x3660, valid JPEG and the Wi-Fi IP. Run start.cmd; open localhost:8865. Video attaches automatically to the paired S3.
  4. For phone/Quest run start-mobile.cmd or start-quest.cmd. Setup is HTTP8868; controls use HTTPS8867. Trust this package's own CA once. Keep the computer running.

Movement and first test

Current configured defaults: output enabled after Arm, 100% drive power, steering hold, 400 ms command timeout OFF. Steering is requested effort, not a measured angle. Avoid sustained end-stop stalls. Emergency Stop and Wi-Fi loss disarm. With command timeout off, the last output can persist if control requests cease while ESP Wi-Fi remains connected.

Raise the wheels. At neutral, Arm, test short drive and steering inputs, return to centre, and press Emergency Stop. Test Wi-Fi interruption and computer shutdown separately. The idle heartbeat cannot refresh motor deadlines, arm the car or replay movement. Keyboard W/S drives, A/D steers, Space stops. Wheel Button0 pauses/resumes inputs. Quest starts in Practice; Live requires fresh video and the grip control.
